What importers in Europe need to know

The main rules that affect a shipment of dehydrated vegetables or spices. Rules change, so confirm the details with your customs broker before you order.

European Union

  • Food must meet EU pesticide residue limits (Regulation 396/2005) and contaminant limits (Regulation 2023/915). Buyers usually ask for a residue report from an accredited lab.
  • Ethylene oxide is not permitted for sterilising food in the EU, so export lots are steam sterilised.
  • Some products from India are on the EU list for extra border checks (Regulation 2019/1793). The list is revised about twice a year, so check it against your product code before each order.
  • Labels and specifications must declare allergens under EU food information rules.

United Kingdom

  • Great Britain has run its own import controls since leaving the EU. Limits and listed products can differ from the EU's, so check both if you sell into both markets.
  • The UK importer is legally responsible for the food meeting UK requirements and must be able to trace it one step back to us.

What buyers here specify

  • Steam-sterilised product with agreed microbial limits.
  • A food safety certificate recognised by retailers, such as BRCGS, IFS or FSSC 22000, from the manufacturer.
  • Lot-by-lot residue and microbiology reports.
  • Organic certification where the product is sold as organic.

Last reviewed November 2026. This is general guidance, not legal advice.
