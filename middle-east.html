What importers in Middle East need to know

The main rules that affect a shipment of dehydrated vegetables or spices. Rules change, so confirm the details with your customs broker before you order.

Saudi Arabia

  • The Saudi importer must register their business and each food item with the Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) before importing.
  • Prepackaged food has to meet the Gulf labelling standard GSO 9, including Arabic text.
  • A Halal certificate is mandatory for food with animal-origin ingredients and for any pack that carries a Halal word or logo. Plant products like ours are often asked for it anyway.

United Arab Emirates

  • Each food item must be registered in the UAE's ZAD food trade system, with its label assessed, before it is imported. The importer does this.
  • Labels need Arabic text and must meet UAE standards. Allow time for registration before the first shipment.

Across the Gulf

  • Production and expiry dates must be printed on the pack by the manufacturer. In markets such as Qatar they cannot be added later with a sticker.
  • Gulf shelf-life standards set expiry periods for many foods, so agree the dates with your importer before we print.
  • Each Gulf state enforces the shared standards on its own timetable. Your clearing agent will know the current practice at your port.

Last reviewed November 2026. This is general guidance, not legal advice.
